Performance emergence in large modelsOnce you pass a certain point, emergence happensThis is very strange.Level emergence: at first only 10%, then suddenly past a certain point performance jumps to 80%Ability emergence: at first it can only do 10 things, then suddenly it can do a great many thingsLanguage Models are Few-Shot Learners🤖"Large language models can learn to complete tasks from just a few examples." Specifically, this paper shows that GPT-3, an autoregressive language model with 175 billion parameters, can perform strongly on a wide range of natural language processing tasks without traditional fine-tuning, simply by being given a few examples in its input (few-shot learning). Key points explained • Few-Shot Learning: the input contains only a few examples (usually 10 to 100); from these examples and the task description, the model can understand and complete the task without any additional gradient updates or fine-tuning. • One-Shot Learning: the model sees just one example and can still complete the task. • Zero-Shot Learning: the model has no examples at all and completes the task from the task description alone.This reduced the reliance on large amounts of task-specific training data and fine-tuning, opening new directions for research and application in natural language processingIt used to be that "pre-training" mattered; now we realise "post-training" matters tooWe used to be shaped by search enginesNow: getting the task done well matters; it doesn't need to respond in secondsThe new consensus: the Scaling Law splits into three: pre-training, post-training, test timeTest-time scaling"reasoning": thinking is needed when the user asks a questionSFT and RLHF in ChatGPTSFT: Supervised Fine-TuningRLHF: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback🤖SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) and RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) in ChatGPT are two key training stages used to improve the model's performance and its alignment with human preferences. SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) • SFT is the first step in training ChatGPT, fine-tuning a pre-trained large language model (such as text-davinci-003 from the GPT-3.5 series). • Training uses a high-quality demonstration dataset carefully written by human annotators, showing how the model should answer questions "correctly". • The goal is for the model to imitate these demonstration answers and learn to generate the expected output; the training is still based on the "next-word prediction" language-modelling objective, only with more supervised data. • Because the demonstration data is limited, the SFT model improves basic abilities but may still fall short in understanding user intent and in alignment. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) • RLHF is the second stage of training after SFT, aimed at further optimising the model to better match human preferences. • Concretely, human annotators rank several answers generated by the SFT model, marking which best meets human expectations, which is used to build a "reward model". • Using the reward model, a reinforcement learning algorithm (such as Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO) optimises the SFT model so that its answers keep improving in quality and in fit with human preferences. • This training lets ChatGPT not only generate reasonable text but also better understand user intent and avoid inappropriate or useless answers. In summary, SFT is supervised fine-tuning of a pre-trained model on human demonstration data, establishing basic answering ability; RLHF then further fine-tunes the model through human feedback and reinforcement learning so its output better fits human expectations and preferences, improving ChatGPT's usefulness and safety.ChatGPT used only 100,000 pairs for trainingThis is also related to "emergent abilities" and the "few-shot learner" — very uncanny

(Long) Chain-of-Thought (CoT) + RLAIFChain-of-Thought: CoTRLAIF: Reinforcement Learning from AI FeedbackThese are two key technologies driving the performance and alignment of large language models (LLMs); combined, they offer a new paradigm for model training.

🤖Below is a technical analysis and the synergies:

  1. The core role of Chain-of-Thought (CoT)Chain-of-thought is a technique that improves a model's ability to handle complex tasks by explicitly showing the reasoning process. Its core features include:Step-by-step reasoning: the model breaks a problem into intermediate steps (such as working through a maths problem step by step), imitating the human logical thinking process.In-context learning: prompts guide the model to generate answers with a reasoning path (for example: "Let's think step by step...").Performance gains: experiments show that CoT significantly improves model performance on tasks such as arithmetic and commonsense reasoning, with especially notable effects on large-scale models (such as GPT-3).Example:Input: "Xiaoming has 5 apples, eats 2, then buys 3 more. How many does he have now?"CoT output:

  2. Initial apples: 5 2. Remaining after eating: 5 - 2 = 3 3. Total after buying: 3 + 3 = 6 Answer: 6

  3. The RLAIF technical processRLAIF replaces human annotation with AI-generated feedback to optimise the reinforcement learning training process. The main steps include:Generating comparison samples: generating multiple candidate answers to the same question (such as versions differing in helpfulness/safety)[6][8].Generating AI feedback: using a teacher model (such as GPT-4) to assess answer quality and produce preference rankings or reward scores[2][5].Training the reward model: training a Reward Model on the AI feedback to quantify how good each answer is[3][10].Optimising the policy model: iteratively updating the model with reinforcement learning algorithms such as PPO to maximise reward[4][8].Key innovations:Lower labour costs: AI feedback can process massive amounts of data automatically, avoiding RLHF's reliance on human annotation[2][4].CoT improves feedback quality: when generating feedback, the teacher model is asked to provide its reasoning (such as "Why is answer A better than B?"), improving the accuracy and interpretability of the assessment[6][8].

  4. The synergy between CoT and RLAIFCombining the two creates gains at the following stages:Feedback generation: CoT prompts make the teacher model show detailed analysis when assessing answers (for example: "Answer A is safer because it avoids sensitive word X, while answer B may cause misunderstanding Y..."), helping the reward model capture subtle differences[6][8].Policy optimisation: through the reasoning paths generated by CoT, the model can more precisely understand the logic behind the reward signal (such as "reduce subjective statements" or "add fact-checking"), speeding up policy iteration[6][9].Experimental results:In harmless dialogue generation, the RLAIF+CoT model's harmlessness rate (88%) exceeded RLHF (76%) and the baseline model (64%)[5][10].In text summarisation, there was no significant difference in human preference win rates between RLAIF and RLHF (71% vs. 73%)[5][10].

SummaryThe combination of CoT and RLAIF represents a direction of automation and interpretability in large-model training: the former improves model capability through step-by-step reasoning, the latter lowers alignment costs through AI feedback.
