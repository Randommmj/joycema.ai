Rediscovering Franklin's Twelve Virtues

September 8, 2025 · 3 min read

Today an Indian friend said to me: Joyce, I want to pay you a compliment. I always see you working, you're always so full of energy, so full of passion.

So I shared the "13 virtues" Franklin set for himself as a young man — one of which is "always be employed in something useful."

I think it's under the influence of principles like these that many of my current habits took shape. Franklin actually had 13, but there's one I don't fully agree with, so I dropped it. The rest I sincerely hope to practise for the rest of my life.

My Indian classmate's reminder made me think again about how important these 12 virtues are. A reminder to myself — to take these important principles up again.

I'm writing them out to give myself a push.

Franklin's 12 Virtues

  1. Temperance.

Don't eat until you're stuffed.

Don't drink until you're tipsy.

  1. Silence.

Don't say things that harm others or yourself. Avoid idle gossip.

  1. Order.

Put everything back in its place; do each task at its proper time.

  1. Resolution.

Resolve to do what you ought to do, and once you do it, see it through to the end.

  1. Frugality.

Don't spend idle money that does no good to yourself or others; eliminate waste.

  1. Industry.

Treasure your time. Always keep your hands busy with something useful. Cut out everything pointless.

  1. Sincerity.

Don't harm others; don't deceive.

Think openly and fairly; speak truthfully.

  1. Justice.

Never benefit yourself at others' expense; never go near anything that offends heaven and reason; never let go of the duties you owe to the public good.

  1. Moderation.

Avoid extremes. Let forbearance dissolve grudges.

  1. Cleanliness.

Allow no uncleanliness in body, clothing or home.

  1. Tranquillity.

Don't let small things, everyday things or unavoidable things throw you off balance.

  1. Humility.

Imitate Jesus and Socrates.

Benjamin Franklin’s 12 Virtues

  1. Temperance

Eat not to dullness; drink not to elevation.

  1. Silence

Speak not but what may benefit others or yourself; avoid trifling conversation.

  1. Order

Let all your things have their places; let each part of your business have its time.

  1. Resolution

Resolve to perform what you ought; perform without fail what you resolve.

  1. Frugality

Make no expense but to do good to others or yourself; i.e., waste nothing.

  1. Industry

Lose no time; be always employed in something useful; cut off all unnecessary actions.

  1. Sincerity

Use no hurtful deceit; think innocently and justly, and, if you speak, speak accordingly.

  1. Justice

Wrong none by doing injuries, or omitting the benefits that are your duty.

  1. Moderation

Avoid extremes; forbear resenting injuries so much as you think they deserve.

  1. Cleanliness

Tolerate no uncleanliness in body, clothes, or habitation.

  1. Tranquillity

Be not disturbed at trifles, or at accidents common or unavoidable.

  1. Humility

Imitate Jesus and Socrates.
