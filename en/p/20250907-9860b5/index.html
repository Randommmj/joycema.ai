Ron Johnson: A Career in Retail

September 7, 2025 · 3 min read

Case SummaryBackgroundRon Johnson (HBS Class of 1984) created successful retail innovations at Target and Apple and was seen as "the Steve Jobs of retail". In 2011, at the urging of Bill Ackman (an investor holding 18% of J.C. Penney), he became CEO of J.C. Penney with the goal of a complete transformation.

J.C. Penney's predicamentAn old-line department store whose customers were middle-class, price-sensitive suburban mothers. From the 2000s it was squeezed from three sides — discount superstores (Walmart, Target), upscale department stores (Macy’s) and fast fashion (H&M, ZARA) — plus e-commerce (Amazon), and its market share and profitability kept sliding.

Johnson's transformation measuresPricing: scrapped 600 annual promotions and introduced "Fair and Square Pricing", with no more discounts or coupons.Placement: proposed a "Store within a store" concept, like a small town's main street plus square, to create an experience.Product: brought in designer brands and exclusive products and reinvented the private labels.People (culture): eliminated sales commissions and adopted Apple "Genius Bar"-style advisory service.Promotion: a new logo, a new spokesperson (Ellen DeGeneres), and slogans in the colours of the American flag.

ResultsImplemented too fast and without testing; loyal customers were disappointed (they were used to coupons), and new customers never became loyal. In 2012 the company posted heavy full-year losses, the share price fell from US$24 to US$16, sales dropped 20%–27%, and thousands were laid off.

OutcomeIn April 2013, Johnson was forced out and former CEO Ullman returned. The board felt that while the direction of the transformation was visionary, its execution was far too aggressive and bordered on disaster.
