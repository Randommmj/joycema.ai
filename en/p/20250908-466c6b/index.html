On Longevity

September 8, 2025

Over the weekend I attended a conversation on anti-ageing moderated by Professor Huang Jingsheng. It was excellent.

Here are a few key points I noted down:

  1. Some re-used drugs really do work for anti-ageing — using low doses of "disease-treating drugs" to manage ageing. - For example, sitagliptin + metformin - Semaglutide. However, it's not recommended for young people under 50. What's good for older people isn't necessarily useful for young people, and may even be harmful.

  2. You can't rush longevity - Overdoing it may lead to cancer - and accelerate ageing

  3. The three pillars of anti-ageing: lose weight, lower blood sugar, lower blood lipids

  4. What do you do when Mum keeps secretly skipping her pills because she thinks "all medicine is a bit poisonous"? - Injections! One shot every six months.

  5. Anti-ageing is like military strategy - Many wellness methods are aggressive and offensive. - If a city has few defending troops, it should stay on the defensive; - if it has strong troops and fine generals, it can go on the attack; - the defending troops are your immune cells

  6. Muscle can make your brain smarter, too - Racquet sports are all excellent for longevity

  7. Latest research! Vibration at a frequency of 40 Hz (sound or light) can reduce Alzheimer's - 1,000 Hz modulated down to 40 Hz

  8. Is telomere length irreversible? - What's measured is the average length - When the proportion of new cells is high, the average telomere length goes up - COVID causes massive inflammation — you need to calm the inflammation

  9. Extend your health spend - The money spent in the last two years of someone who dies at 75 is far more than in the last two years of someone who lives to 100

  10. The goal isn't never dying, but "dying well" - How do you live to a hundred, then switch off? - Die young at a very old age
