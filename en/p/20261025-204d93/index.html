The Attribute Map

Draw where you're willing to be worse

October 25, 2026

Of everything in Frances Frei's operations class, what I come back to most often isn't a single case. It's a very plain chart.

Down the left side: what the customer cares about, ranked from most to least important. Across the top: five steps, from "worst in class" to "best in class." For each attribute you mark how you actually perform, then join the dots.

The chart is not hard to draw. It is hard to draw honestly.

Most companies draw a line that hugs the right edge: decent at everything, wanting to be a little better at everything. But a line that is "pretty good" everywhere usually means there is no position at all. The customer can't say why it has to be you.

The line worth having is slanted. It pushes all the way right on what the customer cares about most, and it is willing to fall back to the left on what they care about least. Whatever that saves goes into the top few rows.

Being worse somewhere isn't a mistake. It's a choice — as long as you know why you're worse there, and it happens to be where the customer cares least.

So I built the little tool below. Enter your company and your target customer, list what they care about, put it in order, and rate yourself. The map draws itself, and underneath it a few notes point out where the trade-offs look right and where the effort may be going to the wrong place.

Try it on your own company, or on a case from class. When you're done, download the map as an image or copy a link to send to a classmate.
