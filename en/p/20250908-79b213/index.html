Case SummaryMay 2008: Christine Day is about to take over as CEO of Lululemon. She had spent 20 years at Starbucks, finally running its Asia-Pacific business.Lululemon backgroundFounded in 1998, the company sells "yoga-inspired athletic apparel." In 2005 it was still a US$40 million startup with 20 stores; by 2008 it was a US$350 million public company with close to 100 stores and 3,000 employees. The board's goal: keep opening stores, push towards US$1 billion in sales, and launch e-commerce at the same time.ChallengesPoor site selection and high build-out costs for new US stores; a chaotic inventory system; weak cross-department collaboration and falling organisational efficiency. The culture requires every employee (executives included) to work 8 hours a month in a store to stay close to customers.Growth performance2007 sales of US$275 million, up 85% year on year; profit of US$31 million, up 300%; US$344 million raised in the IPO; store sales of US$1,710 per square foot, far ahead of Abercrombie & Fitch and J.Crew.Market challengesInventory shortages led to extra air-freight costs; American consumers' spending power weakened, and the share price fell from US$60 to US$31.Brand storyFounder Chip Wilson, unhappy with conventional athletic wear, designed quick-dry, breathable yoga pants and promoted them through yoga teachers acting as brand "ambassadors."The new CEO's missionExpand into new categories such as running, swimming and cycling; fix inventory, site selection and collaboration; preserve the culture and the connection with customers; avoid the fate of a "trendy brand that burns out fast."
